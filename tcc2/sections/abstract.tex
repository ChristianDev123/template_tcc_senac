\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
 \begin{otherlanguage*}{english}
   This study aims to propose a comparative analysis between two processing methods for \gls{smart_metering} systems: 
   \gls{edge_computing}, featuring local \gls{ocr} inference on the \gls{esp32cam} microcontroller via a supervised machine learning model,
   and \gls{cloud_computing}, which transmits the captured image for remote processing using the Tesseract engine alongside the OpenCV library.
   The motivation stems from the high cost of replacing widespread conventional meters in residential and private environments with native smart models,
   making \textit{\gls{retrofitting}} a more economically viable alternative. Data transmission between the edge nodes and the central 
   gateway is conducted over a \gls{lorawan} network using a Spreading Factor of SF8—a configuration that ensures compliance with the 
   400~ms regulatory \textit{dwell time} limit established by ANATEL Act No.~14.448 while maximizing range within this constraint. 
   The feasibility of transmitting fragmented images via \gls{lorawan} was demonstrated analytically: a 160$\times$120 pixel grayscale image in 
   JPEG format (approximately 3.10~KB) can be transmitted across 38 packets in about 13.3~s of active radio time, consuming only 1.5\% of the daily 
   budget under the most restrictive regulatory scenario (EU868 with a 1\% \textit{duty cycle}). The two processing workflows will be evaluated across 
   four quantitative metrics: \gls{ocr} reading accuracy (\%), end-to-end latency (ms), energy consumption per reading (mAh),
   and transmitted data volume (bytes/reading). The results are expected to guide the most appropriate architectural 
   choice for large-scale \textit{Smart Metering} deployments by balancing energy efficiency, 
   recognition accuracy, and system maintenance complexity.
   \vspace{\onelineskip}

   \noindent
\textbf{Keywords}: Smart Metering. ESP32. LoRaWan. OCR.
 \end{otherlanguage*}
\end{resumo}
