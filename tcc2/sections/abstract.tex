\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
 \begin{otherlanguage*}{english}
    This work aims to propose a comparative analysis between two processing architectures for
    analog water meter telemetry systems: \gls{edge_computing}, with \gls{ocr} inference performed
    locally on the \gls{esp32cam} microcontroller through a supervised trained machine learning model,
    and \gls{cloud_computing}, with the transmission of the captured image for remote processing by the 
    Tesseract engine combined with the OpenCV library. The motivation lies in the issue of water waste
    in Brazil and the high cost of replacing conventional meters with native smart models,
    making the \textit{\gls{retrofitting}} of analog water meters an economically viable alternative.
    Data transmission between the edge nodes and the central gateway is carried out via a \gls{lorawan} network
    using Spreading Factor SF8, a configuration that ensures compliance with the regulatory dwell time limit of 400~ms
    established by ANATEL's Act No.~14.448, while maximizing reach within this limit. 
    The feasibility of sending fragmented images via \gls{lorawan} was analytically demonstrated,
    where a 160$\times$120 pixel grayscale image in JPEG format of approximately 3.10~KB can be transmitted in 
    38 packets in about 13.3~s of active radio time, consuming only 1.5\% of the daily budget under the most 
    restrictive regulatory scenario (EU868 with a 1\% duty cycle). 
    The two processing workflows will be evaluated according to 
    four quantitative metrics: \gls{ocr} reading accuracy (\%), end-to-end latency (ms),
    energy consumption per reading (mAh), and volume of transmitted data (bytes/reading).
    The results are expected to guide the choice of the most suitable architecture for 
    large-scale \textit{Smart Metering} deployments, considering the trade-off between energy efficiency,
    recognition accuracy, and system maintenance complexity.

   \noindent
\textbf{Keywords}: Telemetry. ESP32. LoRaWan. OCR.
 \end{otherlanguage*}
\end{resumo}
